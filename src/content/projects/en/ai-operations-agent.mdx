## What it is for

A company receives its signed quotes, invoices and complaints by email. Someone reads
them, re-types them by hand into the client records, files the attachments, replies. The
agent does the reading, the extraction and the preparation; the person keeps the decision.

## What it reads out of a message

- **Two inboxes** — Gmail and Microsoft Graph, polled every minute.
- **Classification by type**: signed quote, client invoice, subcontractor invoice,
  complaint, payment notice, spam. Four keywords at the start of a subject line force the
  classification when the sender knows them.
- **PDF attachment extraction**: client, quote number, invoice number, amounts excluding
  and including tax, address, due date, occupant of the property.
- **Scanned invoices**: a PDF with no text layer goes through character recognition — the
  same one already used for purchase orders.
- **Several documents per message** — one email may carry both the signed quote and the
  purchase order; both follow the record all the way through.

The client database is read-only. Every write defaults to a dry run. Anything the agent
would change lands in a proposal queue, and nothing leaves it without a human click.

## What protects the data

- **Nothing ambiguous is written.** Two amounts in one field, a date that does not exist
  on a calendar, a client missing from the recipient's own list: the value is not
  invented, it is copied verbatim into the free-text column of the record.
- **An empty cell cannot mean two things.** Whatever is not written is always stated
  somewhere.
- **The same message processed twice** does not create two records.
- **A read-only pre-flight** checks, before every release, that the target columns still
  exist on the client's side and that nothing has been renamed behind our back.

## What it fills in

Three destination pages, fifty-five columns. Thirty-five are filled automatically. Of the
twenty left: three are out of reach of the service's interface, sixteen belong to the
human work that happens after the email, and one was dropped at the client's request.
