## The problem

Technicians logged their jobs on paper, and the office re-typed them later. Between the
two: lost sheets, duplicated entries, days of delay before anyone at the office knew
what had actually happened on site.

## The constraints

A construction site is not an office. The network drops behind a wall and comes back in
the van. The hand holding the phone is gloved. The screen is read in direct sunlight,
one-handed, while something else is going on.

A consumer-grade app does not survive that. Neither does an app that assumes a request
will succeed.

## The system

An **offline-first** progressive web app with two faces: the technician's phone, and the
office dashboard.

On site: fast capture, geolocated photos, and a questionnaire that adapts to the type of
job rather than asking everything every time. At the office: live view of the crews,
counters, urgent findings, and a PDF report generated from what was entered in the field.

## The architecture

Every write goes into a **queue** before it goes anywhere near the network. The app
treats being offline as the normal case, not the error case — so a dropped signal, a
refresh, or a closed tab costs nothing. The queue replays the moment the connection
returns.

Access is partitioned at the database level with **row-level security**, designed from
the start for more than one company rather than retrofitted later.

## Reliability

**91 unit tests, all green, in 1.4 seconds** — plus browser tests driving the real
interface. Together, 11 test files and 189 declared cases.

The tests that matter are the ones named after a failure that would hurt: the vendor
route cannot be walked out of, the proposals bridge is closed by default and closed
again without a director token, sensitive routes are rate-limited, and security headers
are present on every response.

## Outcomes

Version 1.0.1, 381 commits between June and August 2026.

The property worth stating is the one the tests protect: **no data is lost when the
network drops** — not as an intention, but as a queue that replays and a suite that
proves it still does.

## Limits

There is no usage instrumentation, so I cannot tell you how many jobs went through it,
and I will not invent a number.

The office view was designed for a small team, not for hundreds of concurrent users.

And it is one deployment, for one company — the multi-tenant partitioning is built and
tested, but it has never carried a second tenant.
