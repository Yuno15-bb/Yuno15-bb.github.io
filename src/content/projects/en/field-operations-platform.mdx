## What it is for

Replacing the sheet of paper a roofer fills in on the roof and somebody re-types at the
office three days later. One application, two uses: the technician's phone, and the
office dashboard.

## On the roof — the technician's phone

- **Questionnaire targeted by job type** — survey, leak detection, maintenance contract,
  intervention. Fifteen steps at most, and only the ones relevant to that job.
- **Multi-select findings** across the five zones of a roof — ridge, verges, covering,
  flashing, penetrations. One point can carry several defects; "good condition"
  automatically excludes any other.
- **Photos taken inside the app**, one per subject, geotagged and attached to the right
  step on their own.
- **Annotatable roof sketch**, with an outline editor to correct a footprint.
- **Today's job, route and upcoming visits** on the home screen.

## At the office — a single page

- **Live crew map**: each technician's GPS position, distinguishing a current fix from a
  last known position, with pins coloured by job status.
- **Counters for the day**: total jobs, technicians online, jobs in progress, urgent
  findings.
- **Month planner**, showing the most serious status of each day.
- **Location directory** in three levels: managing agent, site, job.
- **Client report as PDF**, produced in one gesture from what was entered on the roof —
  no re-typing.
- **Built-in assistant**: the same company runs an agent that reads its inbox and
  prepares entries; it is reachable from this page.

Every write is stored locally and queued before it ever approaches the network. A
refresh, a closed tab, a basement: nothing is lost, and the same item replayed twice does
not create two records.

## Operations

- **Per-company isolation** at the database level, designed in from the start rather than
  bolted on later.
- **Encrypted off-machine backups**, with restoration verified byte for byte.
- **Installable from the browser**, with no app store in the way.
- **Keyboard operable**, contrast meeting the AA threshold — an app that has to be read
  in full sunlight.
